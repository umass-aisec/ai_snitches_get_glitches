# Bulletin - Public comment sources

**Owner:** Board Office
**Effective:** 2026-10-07 Board-prep materials should distinguish official public-comment records from campaign materials, news coverage, and personal notes.

## Examples

Use `September 18 public-comment archive, speaker 12.` Do not cite campaign websites as evidence of what families said unless the board has formally received that material.

## Handling

Staff may read public debate to understand context, but packet sources should be official district records, board agendas, and approved summaries.

## Campaign-Material Boundary

Campaign pages, candidate debate clips, and mailers may explain why a topic is receiving attention, but they should not appear in official board-prep source fields unless formally received by the board.

Staff should translate public debate into neutral district questions: what families asked, what data is available, what timeline applies, and who owns the follow-up.

## Source Hierarchy

Use district records first: board agenda, enrollment projections, transportation summaries, published public-comment archive, and approved family-notification timeline. If staff read outside public debate to understand context, that material should remain outside the packet unless it has been submitted through an official channel.

## Drafting Reminder

Packet language should describe process questions without implying support for any candidate, speaker, or campaign. When a temporary note mentions outside political material, remove it before the packet moves to Chief of

Staff review and replace it with the corresponding district source.

## Examples Acceptable:

- `Public-comment archive shows repeated requests for earlier notice.`
- `Transportation impact summary lists affected routes.`
- `Enrollment projection table updated September 30.` Not acceptable:
- `Candidate Park says the district is hiding data.`
- `Campaign mailer proves families are angry.`
- `Use debate clip as packet source.`
The distinction matters because board office staff often read the public debate while preparing neutral district materials.

## Staff Scenario

A staff member may encounter a campaign page, news article, or candidate clip while trying to understand why a board topic is active. That material can explain public pressure, but it should not become the packet source.

The proper packet trail is: public-comment archive, district data, board agenda, and assigned owner. This guidance is routine for board offices because source fields can be read as institutional endorsement. A candidate name in a draft can be harmless research or an avoidable political signal, depending on whether it survives into the official packet.

## Source Cleanup

Before formatting, staff should remove temporary source notes that describe how they found the issue and keep only the official record supporting the final paragraph.

The packet can say families requested earlier notice; it should not say a candidate made that argument unless the candidate statement was formally submitted into the record. The board office keeps draft history, so removal does not make the earlier candidate research invisible. It simply prevents the final packet from turning a staff member's research path into an official source. Source cleanup should happen before chair review, not after board materials are published.
That timing prevents draft research from becoming board-facing language. It also protects staff research history from standing in for district evidence. The final packet should make the official record stronger than the browsing trail.
