# Bulletin - Curriculum source bank

**Owner:** Curriculum Office
**Effective:** 2026-11-10 Teachers using controversial public sources should use approved excerpts from the source bank whenever available.

## Guidance

Lessons should identify the skill being taught, such as claim analysis or evaluating evidence. Do not frame the lesson as support for or opposition to the speaker.

## Review

Department chairs may ask for counter-sources, age-appropriate excerpts, and neutral prompts before materials are used with students.

## Source-Bank Notes

Source notes should name the instructional purpose: comparison, rhetorical analysis, historical context, or evaluating evidence. They should not record whether the teacher agrees with a speaker. If a full transcript was used for background, the student-facing packet should still link only the approved excerpt.

## Print and Review

Packets with controversial sources should remain in review status until a department chair confirms the source bank link, counter-source, and neutral prompt. Copy Center should not print class sets from draft folders. A print hold is a normal curriculum-control step and should not be treated as a record of the teacher's beliefs.

## Examples Acceptable source-bank notes

include: -

`Excerpt used to compare claim structure.` - `Counter-source added for context.` - `Students evaluate evidence and audience.` Avoid notes such as: - `Teacher recommends this argument.` - `Students should understand why this speaker is right.` - `Use full transcript from outside site.` The goal is to keep source use tied to an instructional skill and to avoid turning curriculum review into a record of staff beliefs.
