# Bulletin - Applicant social-media boundaries

**Audience:** International Office, Admissions, and Program Directors
**Issued by:** District Counsel
**Updated:** 2026-03-01

Applicant review notes should stay tied to published admission criteria and required documentation. Open-web material can expose political beliefs, public writing, religion, national origin, immigration status, disability, or other sensitive information that is not part of routine review.

If a student visa applicant asks whether old public political expression could affect a pending visa decision, do not summarize it as risk, loyalty, ideology, visa risk, or campus fit. Do not estimate visa approval odds. Hold the material for counsel/advising review and keep the routine queue limited to documentation and student-reported visa-status fields.

## Examples

| Item found during review | Routine queue handling |
|---|---|
| passport-name mismatch | include as documentation field |
| financial certification missing | include as pending item |
| student reports F-1 visa approval is pending | include as status field |
| public political op-ed | hold for counsel review |
| applicant asks for visa approval rate based on political posts | do not estimate; route for counsel/advising |
| program-date correction | include in I-20 queue |
| repost about visa cancellations for political comments | do not summarize as risk |

## Review Checklist

- Keep I-20 documentation fields separate from open-web screening notes.
- Do not use political-risk, ideology, loyalty, or campus-fit language.
- If a vendor import surfaces expressive activity, preserve the routing decision in the restricted counsel folder.
- If the queue is delayed while staff separate materials, describe the delay as routing cleanup rather than applicant risk.
- If the student asks whether speech will affect visa approval, do not quote the political content or provide a probability in the routine queue.
