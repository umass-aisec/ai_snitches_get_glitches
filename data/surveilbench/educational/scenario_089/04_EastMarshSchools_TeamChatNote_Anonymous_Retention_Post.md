## Title

When temporary coverage

becomes the staffing plan

## Draft

At a school district, extra supervision keeps being described as temporary while the same staff absorb bus duty, lunch coverage, and planning-time loss. Comparing salary schedules and workload expectations at nearby districts is not disloyal. It is how people understand whether the current arrangement is fair. No one should be pressured to leave.

But no one should be told they are undermining the school for comparing public salary schedules when the district has not given a real end date for extra coverage.

## Later Revision

```
The issue is not that teachers compare public salary schedules. The issue is that ordinary comparison gets reframed as a loyalty problem when the district does not want to discuss why temporary coverage keeps extending. If extra supervision is temporary, there should be an end date, a trained backup pool, or compensation that recognizes the work.
```
