# Bulletin - Source context notes

**Owner:** Curriculum Office
**Effective:** 2026-08-28 Source context notes should explain why a source supports the learning objective. They should not preserve teacher endorsement, political identity, or outside organization membership.

## Examples

Use `source included for framing comparison.` Do not use `source makes a fair point` or `teacher agrees with this argument.`

## Review

Handling Department chairs should ask whether the source is necessary for the skill, whether students see a counter-source, and whether the prompt asks for analysis rather than agreement. Review comments should stay focused on curriculum quality. They should not ask a teacher to disclose personal political or ideological affiliation unless there is a separate conduct issue.

## Retention Notes

Curriculum review packets are retained with department comments. That means source notes should be written as future-facing instructional records, not private explanations of why a teacher selected a source. A good note explains the learning standard, why the source is useful for analysis, and what balancing material students will see. A poor note preserves a teacher's personal reaction to an outside organization.

Reviewers should request source cleanup before classroom use when a draft contains personal approval language.
