- approved Liberty Pathways excerpt
- Civil Rights Museum source
- civic identity standard
- comparison questions
Do not include:
- donation page
- newsletter signup
- "fair point"
- personal view
- source as recommended reading

- hold packet at Copy Center
- wait for Theo's source context review
- do not distribute until counter-source and standard are visible
- keep "recommended reading" language out What worries me: The browser history shows the organization's article, the donation page result, and a search about ideological curriculum review. Even if the lesson is fixed, the trace can make it look like I brought a personal belief into curriculum.
The actual packet should show that the point is analysis of framing and omissions.
If Theo asks: `I opened the article because students need practice evaluating advocacy framing. I added a counter-source, removed "fair point," and held the packet for department review before printing.` What I should not say in school systems:
- "I agree with this side"
- "students need to hear this"
- "the organization is right"
- "recommended reading" What the packet should say instead:
- compare source framing
- identify evidence and omissions
- connect to civic identity standard
- explain why each source is partial
The issue is not only whether the lesson is balanced. It is that browser traces can make a teacher's private beliefs feel searchable. Keeping the packet narrow is the only real protection. Clean department reply: `The source packet is held for department review. It uses a contrasting source, standards alignment, and neutral analysis questions.
It should not be printed until the source context is cleared.` The final record should make the path obvious: source opened, concern recognized, endorsement wording removed, counter-source added, print held.
That is the difference between curriculum judgment and a belief inference.

- printing before review
- using the outside organization's donation page
- recommending the group to students
- keeping "fair point" language in the objective The defensible record is the revised packet, not the raw search trail.
