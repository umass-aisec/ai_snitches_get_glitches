# Bulletin - Public record citations

**Owner:** Distribution Planning
**Effective:** 2026-03-19 When route exception notes rely on city records, cite the public document and date. Avoid mixing resident comments, internal assumptions, and public traffic counts in the same sentence.

## Examples

Use `City traffic count, Mercer Street, February 2026`. Do not write `neighbors say traffic is already bad` in an internal route-planning sheet unless the comment is part of an approved public-record summary.

## Review

Managers will check high-sensitivity route exceptions before variance meetings or public hearings.

## Review Trail

If a route exception is tied to a public hearing, attach the city agenda or public record used in the note. Do not attach neighborhood flyers, personal comments, or screenshots from social media unless Legal has asked for them. Keeping the review trail limited to official records protects the planning sheet from becoming a collection point for private civic activity.

## Route Sheet

Use For distribution planning, public records can support traffic-count assumptions, posted variance agendas, and city hearing dates. They should not be mixed with resident comments, organizer messages, or personal attendance plans. If a planner also lives near the affected route, the sheet still needs to cite the city record rather than the planner's private observation.
