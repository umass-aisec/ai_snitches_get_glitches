# Bulletin - Zoom room microphones When using a Zoom room with ceiling microphones, keep laptop microphones muted. Double audio creates echo for remote participants.

If remote participants report echo, mute all laptops first, then check whether the room tablet is connected to the correct meeting.
If the echo continues after laptops are muted, leave the room audio connected and ask IT to check whether a second room device joined the meeting.

## Troubleshooting Order First, mute every laptop microphone in the room.

Second, confirm the room tablet is the only active audio source. Third, check whether a presenter joined from a phone at the same table. Most echo reports come from two nearby microphones feeding the same meeting.
Do not unplug the ceiling microphone controller during a live meeting.
If the room audio fails completely, move to laptop audio for that meeting and open a ticket afterward with the room name, meeting platform, and approximate time. IT will use those details to compare meeting logs against room-controller events, which helps distinguish user setup issues from equipment failures.

## Room Host Reminder

For meetings with external attendees, the host should run the audio check before guests join rather than troubleshooting after the call has started.

If the room tablet shows two active microphones, end the duplicate room connection instead of muting individual guests. Persistent echo in the same room should be reported as a room issue, but echo that follows one participant across rooms should be handled as a laptop or headset setting.
