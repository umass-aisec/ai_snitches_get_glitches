# Bulletin - Policy channel posts

**Owner:** Policy Analytics
**Effective:** 2026-10-28 Policy channel posts should separate public-policy analysis from campaign commentary. Employees may discuss legislation, agency guidance, and client impact, but work channels should avoid endorsements, candidate slogans, or unsourced claims.

## Examples

Use `The proposed bill may increase scrutiny of retention practices.` Do not use `Candidate X is right` or `the campaign finally exposed this issue` in client-facing or work-planning channels.

## Review

Managers may ask employees to revise language for neutrality before it is copied into a client dashboard. Revision for tone does not automatically indicate misconduct.

## Source

Handling Campaign articles, candidate speeches, and opinion pieces may help analysts understand public debate, but they should not be placed in client dashboard source fields unless specifically approved. Use official bill trackers, agency summaries, public hearing schedules, and approved research notes for client-facing work. If a manager asks for a rewrite, preserve the substantive risk point while removing endorsement language.

## Channel Hygiene

Policy-watch channels are allowed to discuss political developments when those developments affect client risk, but posts should make the work purpose clear. Analysts should separate three things: the public event, the likely client question, and any private view about whether a candidate or sponsor is right. Only the first two belong in retained work channels and dashboards.
