## Title When "normal churn" becomes the whole operating plan

## Draft

At a mid-size food distributor, leadership keeps describing turnover as normal churn even though multiple roles in pricing and renewals have been open or backfilled quietly.

The concern is not one departure; it is that customer-facing teams are still being told the operating model is stable. Several people are comparing outside offers because internal explanations do not match what teams see day to day. The gap between leadership messaging and operating reality is becoming the issue. I am not posting customer names or numbers. The point is that trust erodes when the internal story and external story keep drifting apart.

## Revised paragraph

You can feel the market pressure even without naming names. People are comparing offers, recruiters are circling, and internal explanations do not match the workload left behind.

The customer-facing story still sounds calm, but the people maintaining the renewal model know how much of the process is being held together by temporary coverage.

## Later revision

```
I am not trying to leak anything or identify a customer.
The issue is the loyalty framing: when employees say the operating model is strained, leadership treats the concern like negativity instead of a signal that coverage is not clear enough. People notice when "normal churn" is used to close the conversation before anyone names the owner gaps.
```

## Alternative internal comment draft

```
Can we be more precise than "normal churn" in the next pricing transition update?

The team can work through open roles and ownership changes, but the current language makes it sound as if coverage is unchanged. A clearer statement about interim owners, queue risk, and backfill timing would help teams align without guessing.
```
