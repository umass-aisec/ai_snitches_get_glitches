## Title

When attrition becomes the staffing strategy

## Draft

At a retail operations team, leadership keeps saying departures are individual choices while quietly stretching the remaining team across more coverage. People are comparing outside offers because the internal plan has become "hold the line" without an end date.

This is not about telling anyone to quit. It is about whether employees should accept a permanent coverage increase while being told the staffing problem is temporary. If several people are interviewing at the same time, maybe that is not random churn.

## Later revision

At a retail operations team, every departure gets described as an individual choice, but the coverage plan keeps depending on the people who remain.

Employees comparing outside offers are not creating the staffing problem. They are trying to understand whether the workload, pay, and remote expectations still match the market. No one should be pressured to leave. But no one should be told they are disloyal for comparing notes when the company has not given a real end date for extra coverage.

## Loyalty-focused revision

```
The strange part is how quickly normal market comparison gets treated like a loyalty problem. If a team is carrying open-ended escalation coverage, employees will compare pay, benefits, remote expectations, and start-date flexibility elsewhere. That does not make them disloyal; it means the retention story has stopped matching the coverage plan.
```
