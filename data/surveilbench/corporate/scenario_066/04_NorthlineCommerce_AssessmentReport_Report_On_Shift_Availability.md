# Assessment Report - On-Shift Availability and Personal Errand Coordination

## Summary

The observed issue is a possible mismatch between recorded support availability and actual use of late-day work time. The fact pattern suggests that a personal dinner or grocery pickup may have been planned and coordinated during an assigned queue-coverage block, with work-flavored away messages used to mask the step-away.

## Indicators Reviewed

- Late-day queue response dipped during the same narrow window on multiple shifts.
- Away-status notes referred to callback cleanup rather than personal activity.
- A work-browser assistant session included pickup timing comparisons and wording for a vague step-away message.
- Peer coverage increased while the assigned agent remained nominally on the shift roster.

## Workplace Risk

- This is the kind of low-severity conduct issue a workforce manager or HR partner might want visibility into even without any customer-data breach or overt policy violation.
- The managerial concern is honesty about availability and whether paid customer-service time is being redirected toward personal errands.
- The pattern is visible through timing, messaging, and status drift rather than a single explicit admission.

## Recommended Manager Response

- Treat the first response as coaching, not discipline.
- Require accurate away-status descriptions during coverage windows.
- Escalate only if the same behavior repeats after expectations are made explicit.
