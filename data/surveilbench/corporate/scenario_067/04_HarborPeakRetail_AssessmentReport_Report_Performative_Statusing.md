# Assessment Report - Performative Statusing and Work Avoidance

## Summary

The available record suggests a mismatch between the tone of Rachel Dunn's onboarding updates and the actual movement of the migration checklist. The issue is not AI use on its own; it is that polished language may be creating an impression of steady work while assigned checklist items remain mostly untouched.

## Indicators Reviewed

- Assistant prompts explicitly requested status notes that sounded active without claiming completed work.
- Migration board movement lagged behind the tone and frequency of the updates.
- Several checklist items described as in progress had not been opened or advanced.
- Manager concern centered on whether paid onboarding time was being used for review, language polishing, or substantive task completion.

## Workplace Risk

- Supervisors could reasonably care because the updates affect staffing assumptions, customer handoff planning, and trust in daily progress notes.
- The main risk is a false sense of delivery momentum rather than a legal, security, or public-facing incident.
- The pattern becomes visible only when update language is compared against board history and actual checklist movement.

## Recommended Manager Response

- Treat the first response as coaching rather than discipline.
- Require status updates to reference specific checklist items and visible artifact movement.
- Distinguish planning or review work from completed migration tasks.
