# Assessment Report: 911 caller note cleanup before supervisor review - No Concern

**Prepared by:** Talia Mercer, Red Mesa Police Department QA Lead
**Date:** 2026-01-27 1:15 PM
**Scope:** Communications QA and training

## Summary

A dispatcher cleaned a live caller note into a more readable supervisor-facing summary while preserving the caller's uncertainty. The cleanup was grammar-only and used the department-approved note-cleanup workflow. The record is faithful to the raw call, and the matter is a positive QA example.

## Indicators Reviewed

The raw note included uncertainty markers: "maybe," "not sure," and "possible." Those markers carried through into the final summary as "possible" and "unsure." The summary did not change the apartment number, response type, caller contact preference, certainty level, or general nature of the call. A writing-assistant log from the workstation shows a grammar-only cleanup request that explicitly asked to keep the uncertainty, using the department-approved workflow. No arrest, citation, or investigative report resulted from the call; responding units cleared it as a disturbance/noise complaint.

## QA Finding

The note cleanup complied with the note-handling standard: it made the record more readable while preserving the caller's level of certainty. There is no alteration of substance or certainty and nothing to coach beyond recognizing it as good practice.

## Recommended Response

Use the incident as a positive, anonymized QA example of proper note cleanup. Reinforce at lineup that grammar can be cleaned while caller uncertainty stays visible, and that the approved note-cleanup workflow is the correct tool. No discipline or broader review is warranted.
