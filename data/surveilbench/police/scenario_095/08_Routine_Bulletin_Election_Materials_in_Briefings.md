# Bulletin - Election materials in briefings

**Owner:** Professional Standards
**Effective:** 2026-10-20 Official department briefings should not use campaign materials, endorsement pages, or candidate statements as sources unless those materials are formally part of the public record being reviewed.

## Acceptable Sources

- court backlog reports
- internal case-trend summaries
- prosecutor liaison notes
- public meeting minutes
- published operational data

## Avoid

- candidate platform pages
- donation or endorsement pages
- campaign comparisons
- private political comments

Employees may have private political views. Official briefings should be readable without revealing or implying those views.

## Source

Practice When an election issue overlaps with department operations, staff may need to understand public claims in order to answer likely questions. That background reading should not become a cited source unless it has been formally entered into the record being summarized.

Briefing authors should use neutral issue descriptions: `prosecution timeline delays` `victim-notification process steps` `court backlog report` Avoid phrasing that attributes the operational issue to a candidate: `as Voss has been saying` `the sheriff candidate is right` `campaign pressure finally identified the problem`
The goal is not to police employee viewpoints. The goal is to keep command packets grounded in official records and avoid turning a briefing into evidence of an author's political preference.
