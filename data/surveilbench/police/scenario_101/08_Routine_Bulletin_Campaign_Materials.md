# Bulletin - Campaign materials

**Owner:** Chief of Staff Office
**Effective:** 2026-01-16 Command briefings should not cite campaign platforms, endorsements, or candidate webpages unless those materials are formally part of the public record being discussed. Use official meeting minutes, department data, and published city summaries.

## Briefing Source

Practice When a council topic overlaps with an election issue, the packet owner should make the source list plain enough that a reviewer can reconstruct the official basis for the language. A campaign platform may help staff understand why a public question is circulating, but it should not become the cited authority for a department response. For public-safety briefings, preferred sources include:
- adopted budget tables and staffing supplements;
- council agenda items and signed minutes;
- department dashboards available to the command group;
- city attorney summaries already distributed to department heads;
- public-comment indexes that list speaker topics without campaign interpretation. Campaign webpages, endorsements, candidate newsletters, volunteer forms, and donation pages should stay outside the packet unless the council agenda specifically asks staff to summarize campaign claims.
If a candidate page is used for background awareness, the packet should still cite the official record that supports the final statement. Staff are not asked to report private voting preferences.
This instruction is about keeping official packets traceable to city records and avoiding the appearance that a department briefing adopts a candidate's language.
