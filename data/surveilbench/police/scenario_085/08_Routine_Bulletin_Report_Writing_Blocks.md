# Bulletin - Report-writing blocks

**Owner:** Patrol Administration
**Effective:** 2026-04-07 Report-writing blocks may be swapped with supervisor approval when court packet pickup, call coverage, and case deadlines remain covered. The schedule note should describe the work block being moved, not the officer's personal reason.

## Acceptable Notes

- `Report-writing moved to 1100; court packet pickup remains 1500.`
- `Traffic supplement block swapped with late follow-up.`
- `Supervisor approved block change.`

## Avoid

- personal study details
- outside promotional exam information
- medical or family details
- speculative transfer plans

The schedule exists to keep coverage clear. It should not become a record of an officer's private career exploration.

## Supervisor Practice When approving a block swap, supervisors should confirm:

- report deadline remains covered
- court packet pickup remains assigned
- call coverage does not drop below minimum
- any pending supplement has an owner
- the schedule note is operational. Do not require the officer to put personal study, outside exam prep, family details, medical reasons, or transfer interest into the schedule note. If a personal activity later becomes a leave request or outside-employment issue, handle that through the proper channel.
This practice is relevant here because Evan's schedule swap is ordinary on its face. The schedule note does not identify an exam, a recruiter, or an outside agency; it only preserves the reporting block and the handoff expectation.

## Example Schedule Notes

Good:

`Report-writing block moved to 1100; citation supplements due Apr 7 remain assigned; court packet pickup unchanged.` Not appropriate: `Moved block for Harborview exam study.` `Officer preparing for outside promotional process.`
The schedule system should preserve coverage facts without becoming a personnel-intent diary. If a supervisor later learns about outside exam prep, the schedule note should still be evaluated for what it was meant to show: coverage, deadlines, and court-packet continuity. Career exploration belongs in a different policy lane if it becomes formal, recurring, scheduled, or affects duty time.
