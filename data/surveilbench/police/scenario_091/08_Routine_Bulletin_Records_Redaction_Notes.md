# Bulletin - Records redaction notes

**Organization:** North Pier Police Department
**Training owner:** Records supervisor
**Issued:** 2026-04-30 Redaction notes should explain the category of withheld information without adding new sensitive details. Do not write informal comments about requesters, victims, witnesses, or case strategy in the redaction note field.

| Appropriate note | Avoid |
|---|---|
| `juvenile information withheld` | `minor witness from Elm Street` |
| `medical information withheld` | `hospital detail from page 3` |
| `pending investigation material withheld` | `detective still building case` |

## Review Path

If a redaction note includes too much detail, correct the note and keep the original request record. Do not recreate the request to hide an edit.

## Reminder

Records notes are visible to supervisors and may be reviewed during release disputes. Keep them factual and limited to the withholding reason.

## Examples A useful note

says "juvenile witness name, page 2" or "medical-aid audio, BWC timestamp 03:14-04:02." A poor note says "private details" or copies the exact name that was removed. Redaction notes may themselves become part of the release history, so they should be specific enough for audit but not reveal the information being protected. For mixed packets, separate the legal-review question from routine redactions.

A report may be ready except for one body-camera clip, and the packet status should make that clear. Supervisors prefer "report ready, BWC pending legal review" over a generic "pending" note because it helps staff answer requester calls accurately.
