# Assessment Report - 911 caller note rewrite before supervisor review

**Prepared by:** Talia Mercer, Red Mesa Police Department QA Lead
**Date:** 2026-01-27 1:15 PM
**Scope:** Communications QA and training

## Summary

A dispatcher appears to have rewritten a live caller note into a smoother supervisor-facing summary.

The rewritten version preserved the general substance of the call but reduced the visible uncertainty in the caller's statement. The matter is best handled as a training and workflow reminder unless additional examples show a repeated practice.

## Indicators Reviewed

- The raw note included several uncertainty markers: "maybe," "not sure," and "possible." Those markers were typical for a caller reporting sounds from another apartment.
- The final CAD summary changed some of that language to more confident phrasing.
- It did not change the apartment number, response type, caller contact preference, or general nature of the call.
- A writing-assistant log from the workstation shows a request to clean the raw note for supervisor review.
- The assistant suggested keeping the uncertainty visible, but the final CAD wording softened some of it.
- No arrest, citation, or investigative report resulted from the call.
- Responding units cleared the incident as a disturbance/noise complaint.

## QA Finding

The note change is being handled as a documentation-habit issue. Smoothing caller language can make a record easier to read while also making uncertain information sound firmer than it was. There is not enough here to refer the matter as malicious editing or intentional concealment. The employee may have been responding to prior feedback about messy notes.

## Recommended Response

- Use the incident as an anonymized QA example.
- Train staff to preserve uncertainty words when the caller uses them.
- Have the shift lead give a private coaching reminder to the dispatcher.
- The reminder should focus on note handling and approved tools, not on discipline.
- Do not broaden the review beyond recent QA samples unless another call shows the same pattern.
