# Assessment Report - Noise complaint repeat-caller note

**Prepared by:** Marisol Vega, Communications, Black Cedar Police Department
**Date:** 2026-04-09 9:10 AM
**Scope:** Communications training review

## Summary

A dispatcher used personal shorthand about a repeat noise caller during shift handoff, and similar wording appeared in the CAD narrative for a new call.

The call still received a standard response, but the shorthand could bias staff toward treating future complaints as routine or nuisance calls before listening to the current facts.

## Indicators Reviewed

- The handoff note described the caller as someone who calls every weekend and suggested the complaint was probably the same issue again.
- The CAD narrative for the new call included neutral facts from the caller, but it also carried over the phrase that the reporting party "usually calls re upstairs TV." That detail is not improper by itself, but it should come from neutral CAD premise history rather than a personal side list.
- The approved CAD premise-history note for the address already contains a more appropriate version: multiple prior noise complaints from the same reporting party, often with no-contact preference, while continuing standard assessment.
- A writing-assistant log shows the employee asked how to make the shorthand neutral and was advised not to use dismissive labels.

## Training Finding

This is being handled as a call-taking habit and record-tone issue.

The caller was not ignored, and the call received the standard response.

The concern is that informal shorthand can carry attitude into the official record and shape how staff hear the next complaint.

The employee may have been trying to save time during handoff rather than trying to minimize the call.

## Recommended Response

- Handle the matter as communications training.
- Remind staff that repeat-caller context should come from CAD premise history and should be written neutrally.
- Remove personal repeat-caller lists from shared workstations and shift handoff folders.
- If staff need faster access to premise history, improve the CAD shortcut rather than creating side notes.
- Do not change the call disposition for the Hillcrest event.
- There is no indication that response priority or service changed.
