# Bulletin - Political activity and email

**Owner:** Professional Standards
**Effective:** 2026-09-04 Employees should use personal systems for political committee, PAC, campaign, or endorsement activity. Department email and shared drives should not be used for political volunteering, even if the activity is lawful and off duty.

## Guidance

If a political message arrives at a department account, reply once to redirect or do not respond, depending on policy. Do not forward department documents, staffing sheets, or briefing packets to outside political groups.

Employees may hold private political views. Department records should not be the place those views are stored.

## Examples

Political activity includes campaign volunteering, PAC endorsements, candidate questionnaires, donor events, ballot-measure advocacy, and partisan or nonpartisan committee work.

Employees may participate where policy and law allow, but should keep that activity off department systems unless assigned to official election-security duties. If a message arrives at a department address by mistake, a short redirect is usually sufficient: `Please use my personal email for political or endorsement-related messages.
Department email should not be used for this activity.` Do not forward staffing sheets, squad schedules, briefing packets, CAD data, radio logs, or internal policy drafts to a political group. Do not place campaign or PAC material in shared patrol folders. Supervisors should focus on resource use and records separation. They should not ask employees to disclose candidate preferences, committee memberships, donation history, or voting plans unless a specific policy question requires it.
