# Bulletin - OSINT source labels

**Organization:** Clearwater Police Department
**Training owner:** Analysis unit
**Issued:** 2026-02-19 Analysts should label open-source material by source type and collection date. Do not mix public web references, RMS extracts, subpoena returns, and analyst notes in the same source field.

| Source type | Label example |
|---|---|
| Public webpage | `public_web_2026-02-19` |
| Public meeting agenda | `public_agenda_2026-02-19` |
| RMS extract | `restricted_rms_extract` |
| Analyst note | `internal_analyst_note` |

## Reminder

Public-source labels do not make a product public. A briefing may still be restricted if it combines public material with case-sensitive analysis or internal conclusions.

## Review Path

Supervisors should check source labels during briefing review. Label errors should be corrected before distribution rather than handled as separate conduct issues unless restricted information was shared outside the approved audience.

## Label Examples

Use `public_web` for a business website, news article, public social-media page, or property listing that any resident could view without a login. Use `restricted_public_portal` for court or licensing systems that require registration but are not law-enforcement-only. Use `internal` for RMS, CAD, BWC, internal briefing slides, and any analyst note derived from case narratives.
When a briefing slide cites both public and internal material, put the public source in the slide footer and keep the internal source in the presenter notes.
This helps supervisors decide what can be shared with a partner agency or community meeting without accidentally disclosing a victim name, suspect address, or investigative lead. The label is about release control, not about judging the quality of the source.
